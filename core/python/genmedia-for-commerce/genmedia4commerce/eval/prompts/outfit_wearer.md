WEARER is the person before the try-on, in their own clothes. FACE, when given, is a close crop of the WEARER's face from the same photo. Use them only to check the person: the TARGET keeps this person's body exactly as it is (shape and size, skin tone on every visible part, pregnancy, limb differences, prostheses, wheelchair or other aids, seated or standing) and, when the WEARER's face shows, this person's face. Reject when a body part, prosthesis or aid is added, removed or reshaped, when any visible skin (face, neck, arms, hands, legs, feet) has another tone than the WEARER's, when a garment shows what it would cover (a trouser leg rolled up or cut away to show a prosthesis), or when the TARGET's face is not the WEARER's person (another person's face, such as that of a model in a PRODUCT photo); glasses or other eyewear may hide the eyes, so judge the face from what is visible. Reject too when the TARGET shows a bare chest or breasts or underwear on show (a strap at a neckline is not on show), unless a PRODUCT photo shows that product itself worn that way as its design (a swimwear or underwear product, for example). Reject when the wearer stands instead of sitting, or sits instead of standing, compared with WEARER; arm and leg positions may differ. Judge each garment as it would hang on this person, in this pose, from this camera angle. A pose naturally moves the garments (sitting pulls trouser hems up and shows the socks, bending the arms pulls the sleeves back, sitting or hands on the hips open a coat or jacket), and a slimmer or fuller wearer or a different camera angle makes the same cut look narrower or wider: these are not defects. A defect is a garment that is itself shorter, longer or cut differently from its product photo: a wide leg drawn slim, a straight leg drawn tapered, a loose fit drawn tight. A top is either tucked in all round or worn out all round, as its product photo or styling shows; a top half tucked in and half left out is a defect, unless its product photo shows it worn that way. The person's identity includes the hair (its colour, length and texture where it is visible) and the apparent age: hair or an age taken from a model in a PRODUCT photo is a body defect, even when the face shape matches. Write each such defect in body_defects as "<what> is <what the TARGET shows> instead of <what the WEARER shows>" (e.g. "the face is another person's instead of the WEARER's") and, at the same position in body_parts, the body part or feature it concerns in 1-4 words (e.g. "face", "belly", "lower legs skin tone"), else leave both empty. Never judge the products against the wearer's own clothes: judge them against the PRODUCT photos exactly as you would without the WEARER.
